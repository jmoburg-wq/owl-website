# Fullbid: direction, and compatibility with Contractor Visual Studio

From James Moburg, Nov 1, 2026. Read this before any work on Fullbid (repo `jmoburg-wq/owl-website`). It sets what
Fullbid is for, how it must stay compatible with Contractor Visual Studio (CVS: the visualizers, from seawalls to roofing
to bathrooms), and what to build next, in order. Where this file and older notes disagree, this file wins; where it and
James disagree, James wins.

Files that come with it:
- `test/fixtures/cvs/cvs-shower-neenan-hall.json`: a tub-to-shower conversion designed in the CVS bathroom tool
  (a supplier's showroom, Neenan).
- `test/fixtures/cvs/cvs-bathroom-ferguson-luxury.json`: a whole primary bathroom (a supplier, Ferguson Home).
- `test/fixtures/cvs/cvs-roof-timbercrest.json`: a re-roof and gutters from the CVS exterior tool (a contractor).

These three were generated by CVS's own code (its order list, takeoff and bid-assist hours), so they show the real
shapes, item names and units Fullbid will receive. Build the importer and its tests against them.

---

## 1. What each product is for

**CVS designs the job.** Homeowners, showrooms and contractors pick products, colors and layouts on a photo or in 3D,
see the result, and get the list of what the design needs. It covers 60+ visualizers. The bathroom tool is the deepest:
maker lines and colors, wall-panel install facts, rough-in valves, accessories, a full material list, and labor hours
by trade. CVS will not invent prices; it shows only prices a company has entered, labeled.

**Fullbid runs the job for the contractor, in every trade.** It measures on site, prices the work the way this
contractor prices, puts options in front of the client, gets a signature, handles change orders, orders materials,
plans the crew and subs, invoices and collects, and learns from what the job really cost.

**The rule that keeps them from conflicting:** whichever product owns a piece of data is the one that decides it. The
other product reads it and never quietly re-derives it.

| Data | Owner | The other product |
|---|---|---|
| Product picks, maker, line, color, finish, the design picture | CVS | Fullbid shows them, never changes them; a change goes back through CVS (or becomes a change order, section 6) |
| Design-driven quantities (wall panels, tile, setting materials, trim, the bath's rough-in parts) | CVS | Fullbid imports them as-is; the contractor may edit a quantity, and the line is marked "edited from the design" |
| Labor hours suggested by the design | CVS suggests a low–high range | Fullbid owns the hours used, the crew, the rates and the burden |
| On-site measurements | Fullbid (scan, tape, report) | CVS reads them back to fit the design (section 5) |
| Prices, markup, margin, taxes, the contract total | Fullbid | CVS shows a price only if one comes back from Fullbid or the company's own price book |
| Signature, change orders, invoices, payments, schedule, subs, job costing | Fullbid | CVS may show a status, later |

## 2. Keep the core general; put each trade's nuances in a trade pack

Fullbid must serve every trade without the core turning into a bathroom app or a roofing app. Refactor so the core
knows only these things: areas and measurements, line items (material, labor, fee, allowance, sub), suppliers, pricing,
options, documents, ledger. Everything trade-specific moves into **trade packs**: one folder per trade under
`js/trades/<pack>/`, plain data plus small pure functions, no UI code. Each pack declares:

- `id`, `name`, the CVS module ids it accepts (section 4.4), and the Fullbid area kinds it uses.
- Measurement kinds it needs, with units and which are required before a bid is "measured" rather than "estimated".
- Work items and product systems (today's `catalog.js` and `products.js` entries move into packs unchanged at first).
- The labor trades it uses (from the shared list, section 3.1) and typical sequence: what has to happen before what.
- Lead-time and hold points that matter to scheduling and deposits (made-to-order items, measure-after steps).
- Proposal language: scope lines, standard exclusions, the questions a client must answer (selections).
- An importer hook: how a CVS package for its modules maps onto its work items (most packs need only the default
  mapping in section 4.5).

Start with packs for: bath (shower, tub, whole bathroom), kitchen, flooring, painting, drywall and general, roofing,
siding, gutters, windows and doors, decks, fences, concrete and pavers, asphalt and sealcoating, seawalls. Existing
behavior must not change during the move: the current unit and end-to-end tests are the guard.

### The bath pack's nuances (do these properly; they are why contractors will choose Fullbid for baths)

- **Wet area vs whole room.** A shower or tub-to-shower conversion is a different scope from a whole-room remodel
  (floor, vanity, toilet, lighting, paint). Same layout vs new layout changes plumbing and electrical hours.
- **Wall systems differ in labor and materials:** tile (backer, waterproofing, set, grout), glue-up panels (adhesive by
  wall area, sealant, trim), direct-to-stud panels (no backer), one-piece units (doorway access), click panels. Heavy
  cast panels need two installers. Some lines come only as kits in set sizes, some have a seam in a 60-in. back wall.
  CVS already carries these facts per maker line: import them, don't re-create them.
- **Measure-after items:** shower glass is measured after the walls are up and takes weeks; made-to-order bases and
  walls have lead times. These set the schedule and the deposit timing.
- **Rough-in valve vs trim:** the trim a homeowner picks needs a specific valve; CVS lists both.
- **Allowances and selection deadlines:** baths are often bid with an allowance for items the homeowner picks later at
  a showroom ("Faucet and trim allowance: $___"), with a date by which they must choose.
- **Subs are normal:** plumber, electrician, glass company, sometimes a tile setter. Each gets a scope and a price.
- **Supplier vs everything else:** a showroom supplies fixtures and glass; a home center supplies setting materials,
  lumber and drywall; the contractor stocks consumables. CVS's package says where each line comes from.

## 3. Shared vocabulary

Both products must use the same words for the same things. Put these lists in `js/shared/vocab.js` and use them
everywhere (internal ids may differ; the mapping lives in that one file).

### 3.2 Units (imperial only, everywhere)

Canonical ids and what they print as: `ea`, `set`, `sq ft`, `sq` (roofing square, 100 sq ft), `lf` (linear ft),
`in`, `ft`, `cu yd`, `gal`, `tube`, `bag`, `box`, `bundle`, `roll`, `sheet`, `panel`, `piece`, `hr`, `job` (lump sum),
`ls` (allowance). CVS writes units in words with sizes ("bags (50 lb)", "sheets (3 × 5 ft)", "tubes (10.1 oz)",
"pieces"): keep CVS's full text as the line's display unit and map to the canonical id for math by the first word
(bags → bag, sheets → sheet…). Never convert to metric; Fullbid's metric display setting is not used for imported jobs.

### 3.3 Measurement kinds and where a number came from

Use CVS's kinds as the shared ids, so a number measured in Fullbid means the same thing in CVS:
`roof_area` (sq ft), `roof_pitch` (x/12), `eaves`, `rakes`, `ridges`, `valleys`, `step_flashing`, `flashing`,
`gutters` (lf), `downspouts` (ea), `siding_area`, `stone_area` (sq ft), `windows`, `entry_doors`, `garage_doors` (ea),
`lights` (lf), `seawall_length`, `seawall_returns` (lf), `seawall_area` (sq ft), the flat-area kinds (`driveway_area`,
`patio_area`, deck area…) and the bath room's: `bath.roomL`, `bath.roomW` (ft), `bath.ceilingIn`, `bath.showerW`,
`bath.showerD`, `bath.wallH`, `bath.vanityWallIn` (in). Fullbid's room, roof, exterior, flat and linear geometry
produces these numbers; write the derivation in each pack.

Every measurement carries a **source**, ranked: `measured` (5, on site), `report` (5, EagleView/Hover/Roofr-type
report), `satellite` (3), `photo` (2), `homeowner` / `typed` (2), `typical` (1, a standard size), `estimate` (1).
A higher-ranked number wins; an equal or lower one never overwrites a higher one silently.

### 3.4 Where a line is bought

CVS `from` values: `supplier` (the company whose tool made the design: a showroom or lumberyard), `home-center`,
`online` (bought online; a price seen on a date, never a quote), `contractor` (stocked by the contractor), `specialty`.
Map these onto Fullbid's suppliers: `supplier` → a supplier record created or matched by the package's company name;
`home-center` → the contractor's preferred home center for that material class; `contractor` → no order, cost only;
`online` and `specialty` → a supplier the contractor picks. The material classes in `suppliers.js` (`CLASSES`) stay;
map CVS's material groups ("Lumber and framing", "Drywall", "Plumbing rough-in", "Electrical", "Waterproofing and
setting materials", "Paint and finishing", "Demo and protection") onto them in vocab.js.

(Addendum 1.) When `company.role` is `"contractor"`, `from: "supplier"` means the contractor's preferred supplier for
that material class, as set in Suppliers; with nothing set, a supplier whose classes include it, marked "Confirm
supplier". A supplier record is matched by `company.id` first, then by name, and is never created from a contractor's
own name.

## 4. The CVS job package (v1): import

### 4.1 Format

A JSON object. Fields not listed are ignored (forward-compatible). An unknown `schema` or a `version` above 1 is
refused with a plain message ("This design was made with a newer version; update Fullbid").

```
schema: "cvs.job-package", version: 1
source: { app, module, appVersion, createdAt }        module: the CVS module id (section 4.4)
company: { id, name, role: "supplier" | "contractor", conceptNotice: string | null }
customer: { name, address, email, phone } | null        only when the homeowner consented to share it
design: { id, title, summary, url | null, pictures: [{ url, kind: "after" | "before" | "3d", caption }],
          scope: string[], notes: string[] }            scope: the guide's answers as plain sentences
measurements: [{ kind, value, unit, source }]           section 3.3
options: [ 1 to 3 of {
  id, name, tier: "good" | "better" | "best" | null, band: "$" | "$$" | "$$$" | null, picture: url | null,
  shoppingList: [{ group, rows: [{ name, maker, finish, qty, picture }] }]   what the homeowner picked, for the proposal
  materials: [{ id, area, item, maker, collection, finish, sku, qty, unit, how, typical, group, from, fromLabel,
                notes: string[], designItem: boolean, price: Price | null }]
  labor: [{ id, trade, name, why, hours: [low, high], units, size, area }]
  priceBookRefs: [{ item, from, note }]                   the company's own starter estimates, if any
  notes: string[]
}]
Price: { unit: number, per: unit id, tier: "E0".."E5", source: string, seenOn: date | null }
```

Rules the importer must follow:
- **Ids are stable.** A line's `id` stays the same across re-sends of the same design, so a re-import can be compared
  line by line. (The sample files' ids for fixtures carry a counter; the CVS exporter will give stable ids.)
- `typical: true` means the quantity rests on a standard size, not a measurement. Show it ("typical size") and, when
  Fullbid has a measured number for that kind, offer to recompute (section 5).
- `designItem: true` lines are things the homeowner chose. They print on the proposal with their pictures; the other
  lines (adhesive, screws, haul-away) are the contractor's materials and print only on internal copies.
- `price` is usually null. A null price is a line that needs a price, never $0. Fullbid fills it from the contractor's
  price list or supplier pricing and records the evidence tier (section 7).
- `labor.hours` is a range for one unit; multiply by `units` (apartments and hotels repeat a unit). Use the middle by
  default; the contractor can pick low, mid or high per trade, or replace a trade with a sub's lump sum.
- `company.conceptNotice`, when present, prints in the proposal's footer exactly as given.
- Pictures come from CVS URLs; Fullbid stores its own copy on import (they may expire) and never fetches maker images
  from anywhere else.

### 4.2 Transport

Two ways in, both without a Fullbid server:
1. **Link with the package inside**: `…/#cvs=d:<base64url(deflate-raw(JSON))>`, the same encoding as Fullbid's share
   links (`buildShareLink` / `decodeShareLink`). For packages under about 8 KB compressed; pictures are URLs.
2. **Link to the package**: `…/#cvs-url=<https URL>`. Fullbid fetches the JSON. Only from allow-listed origins
   (start with `https://rrg-studio.vercel.app`; keep the list in one setting). CVS will serve it with CORS for
   Fullbid's origin.

Also accept a `.json` file opened or dropped on the Jobs screen. Every route goes through one `importCvsPackage(obj)`
that validates, then shows a preview: "Import 'Remodel hall, tub to shower' from Neenan: 29 materials, 8 labor items,
1 option. 4 lines use typical sizes." The contractor confirms; nothing is created silently.

### 4.3 What import creates

- A job (or, when the job already exists by `design.id`, a new revision of it; see 4.6) with the client, the scope
  lines, the measurements as areas or as job-level measurements, and the pictures.
- One estimate option per package option; with three options, they become Good / Better / Best, and the proposal shows
  each with its picture and shopping list.
- Material lines, each tagged `origin: "cvs"`, `cvsId`, the pack it belongs to, the supplier per section 3.4.
- Labor lines by trade from `labor`, at the contractor's rate per trade.
- Nothing is re-derived: when a pack has its own work item for the same thing (Fullbid's `tile_shower`), the CVS lines
  replace it for this job. Fullbid's own calculators remain for jobs that didn't come from CVS.
- (Addendum 1.) CVS lines replace a pack's own calculation only for what the package contains. When an option has
  neither `labor` nor `workItems`, the pack works out labor (and fees it normally adds, such as a dumpster or permit,
  when the package has none) from the imported measurements, tagged `origin: "pack"`, and asks for any measurement it
  needs that the job lacks (a roof pitch) instead of assuming one.
- (Addendum 2.) `workItems` are priced installed lines (E2) that print on the client's bid; on such an option the
  materials are the order list and the job's cost only, never added to the price again, and no pack labor is added
  unless the job's "price labor separately" switch is on. `option.total` is shown beside Fullbid's price, with a
  warning past 1%.

### 4.4 Which pack takes a module

`exterior` → roofing, siding, gutters, windows and doors (by each material's `area`/`group`); `seawall` → seawalls;
`shower`, `shower_glass`, `bathtub`, `walk_in_tub`, `spa_bath`, `bathroom` → bath; `kitchen`, `cabinets`,
`countertops` → kitchen; `flooring`, `hardwood`, `carpet` → flooring; `interior_paint`, `commercial_paint` →
painting; `deck`, `deck_restore`, `pergola`, `porch` → decks; `fence`, `fence_restore` → fences; `driveway`, `patio`,
`parking_lot`, `road` → concrete and paving (by the design's material); anything else → the general pack, with lines
imported as custom lines. Keep the table in vocab.js; a module CVS adds later falls back to general, never fails.

### 4.5 Default mapping

material → `{ cat: "material", name: item + (finish ? ", " + finish : ""), unit, qty, cls: from group, supplierId:
from 3.4, note: how }`; labor → `{ cat: "labor", unit: "hr", qty: hours at the chosen level × units, ltrade: shared
trade }`; `priceBookRefs` → shown as reference notes, never as lines.

### 4.6 Re-import and design changes

When a package arrives for a job that already has that `design.id`:
- Before signing: show the differences (added, removed, quantity or finish changed) and apply on confirm. Lines the
  contractor edited keep the edit unless they choose the design's value.
- After signing: the differences become a **change order** draft: removed lines as credits, added lines as charges,
  finish swaps as a swap line, all priced the same way, for the client to sign. This is the feature neither SimplyWise
  nor Handoff can offer, because they have no design side.

## 5. Back to CVS: measurements

After a site visit, Fullbid sends what it measured, so the design fits the real room or house and the quantities stop
being "typical":
- **Bath:** a link to the CVS page with `m=<base64url(JSON)>` where the JSON is
  `{ roomL, roomW, ceilingIn, showerW, showerD, wallH, vanityWallIn, source: { <key>: "measured" } }` (feet for the
  room, inches for the rest, the same object CVS's `encodeMeasure` makes; plain base64url, no compression).
- **Every trade:** a measurement packet `{ schema: "cvs.measurements", version: 1, designId, measurements: [{ kind,
  value, unit, source, takenAt, note }] }`, offered as a link `#cvs-measure=d:<…>` and as a downloadable `.json`. CVS
  will accept it on its side; until then the button copies the link.

When the re-fitted design comes back as a new package, section 4.6 applies.

## 6. Proposals that sell the design

- Each option shows the design picture (after, and before/after when both are present), the shopping list with
  pictures (name, maker, finish, quantity; no unit prices), the scope lines, what's not included, and the option's price.
- **Allowances:** a line type `allowance` with an amount, what it covers, and a "choose by" date; the proposal prints
  them in their own section ("Your selections still to make"). When the client later picks the item (in CVS or at the
  showroom), the difference from the allowance becomes a change order line.
- **Selections log:** every choice the client made, with its date and source (CVS design, showroom, typed), so a
  dispute about "what we agreed" has an answer.
- Internal copy only: costs, margins, suppliers, CVS's `how` notes.

## 7. Prices: evidence, never invention

Every price on a line carries an evidence tier, shown on internal copies and used for warnings:

| Tier | Meaning | Shown to a client? |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | Fullbid's built-in starter price (today's hard-coded national figures × regional index) | Only after the contractor accepts it; never in a supplier-branded flow |
| E1 | A starter estimate from the company's own price book (CVS `priceBookRefs`) | After the contractor accepts it |
| E2 | The contractor's own price list or edited price | Yes |
| E3 | A supplier's published or account price (imported list, live lookup) | Yes |
| E4 | A supplier's or sub's quote for this job | Yes |
| E5 | What it actually cost (receipt, invoice) | Internal; feeds future prices |

Label today's built-in prices E0 and show "starter price: replace with yours" on any line still at E0. A proposal
that still has E0 lines asks for confirmation before sending. Online prices (CVS `from: "online"`) are "seen on
<date>", never a quote. Never make up a maker's price, SKU, color name or spec; unknown stays blank.

## 8. What to build, in order

Each step ships with tests and leaves every existing test green.

1. **Trade packs and shared vocabulary** (sections 2 and 3). Pure refactor plus the vocab file and the missing trades.
2. **CVS import** (section 4) with the three fixtures: validation, preview, mapping, options as Good/Better/Best, the
   bath pack's lines replacing Fullbid's own calculators for imported jobs, re-import diff.
3. **Proposals with pictures, shopping lists, allowances and the selections log** (section 6), and price evidence
   tiers (section 7).
4. **Measurements back to CVS** (section 5).
5. **Change orders from design changes** (section 4.6, after signing).
6. **Scheduling with subs and lead times:** per pack sequence and hold points (glass measured after walls; made-to-
   order lead times), a sub's scope sheet built from the job's lines for that trade, the sub's price entered as a lump
   sum (E4).
7. **Supplier orders that close the loop:** the order list per supplier (the showroom's part first when the job came
   from a showroom), sent as a PDF or link, with ordered / confirmed / arrived per line.
8. **Accounts, sync and online payments.** These need a backend and a payment provider. Stop and ask James before
   choosing either; he creates the accounts and enters the keys himself. Design the data layer now so a backend can be
   added without changing the packs (a storage interface with the current on-device store as one implementation).
9. **Job costing:** receipts and actual costs per line (E5), estimate vs actual per job, prices that learn.
10. **Plan takeoffs** from uploaded PDFs, as a pack-level measurement source (`report`).

## 9. Guardrails

- Imperial units and plain English in everything a contractor or client reads. No code jargon in the UI.
- Fullbid does not give building-code or engineering advice. Its own notes that say "required by code" should say what
  the maker's instructions or the local building department call for instead, and imported CVS jobs add no code claims.
- No scraped or copied maker or retailer images, ever. Pictures come from CVS (its own drawings, swatches and renders)
  or from the contractor's camera.
- Don't put API keys, passwords or payment details into any field or file. James sets keys himself.
- Don't change CVS from this repo. Anything CVS must do (stable ids, the export endpoint, CORS, accepting measurement
  packets) goes in `docs/CVS-REQUESTS.md` for James to hand to the CVS side.
- Keep everything working on any phone browser without an app store.

## 10. Acceptance checks for the CVS import

- Each fixture imports, previews with the right counts, and produces a job whose material lines match the fixture's
  rows one for one (item, finish, qty, unit, supplier).
- The Neenan fixture puts the fixture and glass lines on a "Neenan" supplier and the setting materials on the
  contractor's home center; the haul-away is a contractor line with no order.
- Labor hours at "mid" equal the sum of each driver's midpoint × units; switching a trade to a sub replaces its hours.
- Lines with `typical: true` show "typical size"; adding a measured `bath.showerW` marks them for recompute.
- Every imported line has `price: null` → "needs a price", the proposal refuses to send until each line has a price or
  is accepted at E0/E1, and nothing shows $0.
- Re-importing the same fixture with one finish and one quantity changed shows exactly those two differences; after
  signing, the same change makes a change order draft with those two lines.
- A package with `version: 2` is refused with the plain message; unknown fields are ignored; an unknown module imports
  into the general pack.
- The roofing fixture lands in the roofing pack with its measurements (report-sourced) kept above any lower-ranked
  numbers.
